# Source-verification matrix

This file records the source standard for the active procedure chapters. It is an audit aid, not a claim that every chapter has already been individually verified. Medical content should be checked against a current patient-facing source and, when relevant, a specialty-society guideline or systematic review before publication.

The book follows the patient-facing structure used by major medical references: what the procedure is, why it is done, preparation, performance, risks and limitations, recovery, follow-up, warning signs, and references. Results are described as clinical interpretation rather than as isolated numeric diagnoses.

## Verification rules

- Use a current NIH/MedlinePlus or other government source for basic patient information where available.
- Cross-check procedure-specific preparation, contraindications, medication instructions, and warning signs with Mayo Clinic, a relevant professional society, or an institutional guideline.
- Never tell readers to stop anticoagulants, antiplatelet drugs, diabetes medicines, or other prescription medicines without a clinician-directed plan.
- Record a direct URL, organization, title, publication/update date, and access or review date in the chapter references.
- Confirm that pregnancy, kidney disease, implanted devices, sedation, and emergency indications are addressed when relevant.
- A clinician with relevant expertise should review high-risk procedures before publication.

## Current audit finding

The chapter files currently contain generic bibliographic entries but do not consistently contain direct URLs, identifiers, or review dates. That is a publication-readiness gap. The matrix is included so each chapter can be upgraded and signed off without treating the generic references as evidence of complete source verification.
