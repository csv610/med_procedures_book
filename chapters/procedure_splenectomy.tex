\chapter{Splenectomy}
\section*{Overview}
Splenectomy is surgical removal of the spleen, performed through an open or laparoscopic approach.
\section*{Common Instruments and Equipment}
The exact setup varies by technique and setting. Common items may include:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item open or laparoscopic abdominal surgery instruments
  \item vascular stapler, clips, or ligatures for splenic vessels
  \item specimen retrieval bag for minimally invasive surgery
\end{itemize}

\section*{Why It Is Done}
Indications include traumatic rupture, hypersplenism, selected blood disorders, splenic tumors, cysts, and severe infection or infarction.
\section*{How It Is Performed}
The splenic vessels are controlled, the spleen is freed from surrounding attachments, and it is removed. Laparoscopic surgery is common when anatomy and urgency permit.
\section*{Contraindications}
No single contraindication applies to every patient; the treating team assesses whether the procedure is suitable for the individual and the reason for treatment.

\section*{Precautions}
The care team reviews relevant health conditions, medicines, allergies, and procedure-specific risks, and explains any changes needed for the individual.

\section*{Risks and Recovery}
Bleeding, infection, injury to nearby organs, blood clots, and lifelong increased risk from encapsulated bacteria are possible. Recommended vaccines and infection precautions are essential.
