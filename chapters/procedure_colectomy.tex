\chapter{Colectomy}
\section*{Overview}
Colectomy removes part or all of the colon and reconnects the bowel or creates a temporary or permanent ostomy.
\section*{Common Instruments and Equipment}
The exact setup varies by technique and setting. Common items may include:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item open or minimally invasive bowel surgery instrument set
  \item stapling or suturing devices for bowel division and anastomosis
  \item specimen bag and ostomy supplies when needed
\end{itemize}

\section*{Why It Is Done}
It may be required for colorectal cancer, diverticular disease, inflammatory bowel disease, obstruction, perforation, ischemia, or severe bleeding.
\section*{How It Is Performed}
The operation may be open, laparoscopic, or robotic. The diseased segment is removed, lymph nodes may be sampled, and the bowel ends are joined when safe.
\section*{Contraindications}
No single contraindication applies to every patient; the treating team assesses whether the procedure is suitable for the individual and the reason for treatment.

\section*{Precautions}
The care team reviews relevant health conditions, medicines, allergies, and procedure-specific risks, and explains any changes needed for the individual.

\section*{Risks and Recovery}
Leak, infection, bleeding, ileus, bowel obstruction, blood clots, injury to nearby organs, and need for an ostomy are possible. Recovery includes gradual diet advancement and monitoring of bowel function.
