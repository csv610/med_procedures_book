\chapter{Nephrectomy}
\section*{Overview}
Nephrectomy removes part of a kidney or the entire kidney. Partial nephrectomy preserves functioning renal tissue when feasible.
\section*{Common Instruments and Equipment}
The exact setup varies by technique and setting. Common items may include:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
  \item open, laparoscopic, or robotic urologic instrument set
  \item vascular stapler or clamps for renal vessels
  \item specimen retrieval bag for minimally invasive surgery
\end{itemize}

\section*{Why It Is Done}
It may be performed for kidney cancer, severe trauma, a nonfunctioning infected kidney, donation, or selected complex renal disease.
\section*{How It Is Performed}
The operation may be open, laparoscopic, or robotic. The renal vessels and ureter are controlled, and the kidney or diseased portion is removed.
\section*{Contraindications}
No single contraindication applies to every patient; the treating team assesses whether the procedure is suitable for the individual and the reason for treatment.

\section*{Precautions}
The care team reviews relevant health conditions, medicines, allergies, and procedure-specific risks, and explains any changes needed for the individual.

\section*{Risks and Recovery}
Bleeding, infection, injury to nearby organs, urine leak, blood clots, reduced kidney function, and need for dialysis are possible. Kidney function and blood pressure require follow-up.
